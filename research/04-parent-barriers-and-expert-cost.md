# Parent barriers and expert-access cost

## Evidence-backed barriers

A 2025 systematic review and meta-synthesis screened 4,440 records and synthesized nine qualitative studies of parent-child shared book reading. Repeated barriers included:

- busyness and fatigue after work;
- competing demands and lack of time;
- parental stress or mental-health burden;
- uncertainty about how to share books effectively;
- uncertainty about choosing appropriate books;
- limited access to books or a comfortable reading space;
- a child appearing uninterested or “not ready”;
- lack of a shared-reading model in the parent's own childhood;
- reading being framed as educational work rather than shared pleasure.

Facilitators included child and parent enjoyment, visible child feedback, child choice, a predictable routine, appropriate book length, rhythm/rhyme, access to materials, and practical parent guidance.

## Why “parents lack time” is still too broad

The first target segment should be narrower:

> Mandarin-speaking working parents of 20–36-month-old children who value advanced language/visual-language development, want to add ASL vocabulary, already use books at least occasionally, and feel too tired or uncertain to consistently create interactive story sessions after work.

This segment is more testable than “all busy parents.” It also avoids assuming that every family values sign language enough to pay.

## Expert-cost reality

The roles are distinct:

- **Deaf ASL educator:** language and cultural authority; teaches natural sign language.
- **Curriculum designer:** sequences learning objectives and story practice.
- **Sign-language interpreter:** interprets between languages; not automatically a toddler educator.
- **Speech-language pathologist:** evaluates/treats communication disorders; not automatically fluent in ASL.
- **Animator/motion-capture specialist:** turns validated performance into a reusable asset.

Relevant U.S. benchmarks:

- Interpreters and translators: 2024 median annual wage `$59,440`; top 10% above `$99,830`.
- Speech-language pathologists: 2024 median annual wage `$95,410`.
- Publicly listed private ASL instruction observed during this review: approximately `$30–$80/hour` depending on provider, credentials, format, and location.

These numbers do not prove that a family would spend `$70,000/year`. They show that continuous one-to-one professional time is structurally expensive relative to reusable expert-authored software.

## Correct pain statement

Avoid:

> Parents need to hire a $70k sign-language person.

Use:

> Families who want consistent, high-quality visual-language instruction face fragmented resources, scheduling friction, uncertain quality, and recurring expert cost. Tired caregivers also need help turning expertise into a short interaction they can actually sustain at home.

## What software can and cannot save

Software can reduce:

- repeated delivery cost of the same validated sign/story;
- planning and book-selection effort;
- uncertainty about the next appropriate concept;
- the activation energy to begin;
- repeated narration effort on low-energy days;
- progress-memory burden.

Software cannot remove the need for:

- expert curriculum authorship and sign validation;
- caregiver presence for a toddler-facing experience;
- human interpretation of ambiguous child behavior;
- clinical evaluation when development is a concern;
- real community exposure if the goal is genuine ASL competence.

## Interview questions to validate the pain

Do not pitch the solution first. Ask:

1. Tell me about the last time you read or told a story with your child.
2. What usually prevents it from happening?
3. What happens on a low-energy evening?
4. What have you tried—books, audio, classes, tutors, apps, grandparents?
5. What did you pay, and what did you stop using?
6. Have you tried teaching ASL signs, and where did the material come from?
7. How do you decide what is too easy or difficult?
8. What would make you distrust a signing avatar?
9. Would a two-minute guided interaction feel helpful or like another task?
10. What evidence would you need before paying?
